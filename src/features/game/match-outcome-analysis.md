# Match outcome system: analysis and realism roadmap

> Historical audit: this report describes the engine before the subsequent
> implementation. See [match-outcome-model.md](./match-outcome-model.md) for
> current behavior, calibration evidence, and remaining limitations.

## Scope and evidence

This is an analysis of the current code, not an implementation plan that has already
been delivered. It covers live deliveries, fielding and tactics, innings and match
results, unattended career fixtures, scorecards, and validation. The code is the
authority where older proposals differ. In particular,
[the earlier settings-impact proposal](./match-engine-settings-impact-plan.md)
predates the current twelve field presets.

Findings below distinguish **observed code behavior** from **unmeasured realism
concerns**. The report does not claim that simulated results match real cricket:
no historical dataset or full-match calibration experiment was run for this review.
Cricket regulations vary by competition and season; a future implementation should
make its ruleset explicit rather than assume every match uses the same regulations.

## Executive summary

The engine already has a coherent, fast, inspectable weighted-outcome loop, and
players can make meaningful tactical choices. Its biggest realism problems are not
simply the probability values: several cricket rules and scorecard relationships
are inconsistent, the user-controlled and computer-controlled sides do not use
the same tactical model, Test cricket is represented as a limited-overs match, and
unattended career fixtures are generated by a separate synthetic process.

Recommended order:

1. Correct free hits, over completion, dismissals, extras, result handling, and
   scorecard invariants before tuning outcome rates.
2. Introduce structured delivery events and a reproducible random source, then
   use them for live play, replay, and automated evaluation.
3. Give both sides tactics and make field coverage interact with where the ball
   actually goes, rather than only a selected preset or intended target.
4. Add continuous player, delivery, situation, and condition effects; support
   format-specific rules; then calibrate against documented, stratified data.
5. Version any behavior change and preserve the current arcade mechanics and
   existing saved games unless the player explicitly chooses a different ruleset.

## How outcomes currently work

### Match and innings flow

The controller selects the batting and bowling sides, current striker and bowler,
and which side the player controls. It resolves a delivery, updates score and
statistics, handles replacement batters and over changes, records a short history,
and advances the innings or match as appropriate
([delivery engine](./hooks/controller/controllerDeliveryEngine.js),
[innings controller](./hooks/controller/controllerInningsCore.js)).
Career control can depend on whether the created player is the striker or bowler,
so control may change during an innings.

Normal innings are bounded by legal balls and ten wickets; a chase can end when the
target is reached. Match formats are configured in [matchTypeList.js](../../gameData/matchTypeList.js).
The result and tournament paths use score-based helpers in
[simulatorUtils.js](../../utils/simulatorUtils.js) and
[controllerTournamentUtils.js](./utils/controllerTournamentUtils.js).

### Distribution and sampling

[controllerOutcomeUtils.js](./utils/controllerOutcomeUtils.js) starts with
format-specific weights for `0`, `1`, `2`, `3`, `4`, `6`, `wide`, `nb`, and `W`.
Near the end of a limited-overs innings, it switches to the preceding, more
aggressive format table, rather than smoothly changing risk with match state.
The model applies:

- Pitch-adjusted batting and bowling abilities, with pace or spin selected from
  the bowler's stronger rating; weather and a home bowling adjustment.
- Outfield `boundaryScoring`, captain and vice-captain effects, and additive
  active player and team skill effects.
- Applicable user batting or bowling tactics and, when the user bats, AI field
  coverage in the intended target sector.

Negative values are clamped, values are rounded to integer slots, and a free hit
can suppress `W`. If non-dot slots total at most 100, dots are replaced by the
remaining slots. Otherwise all slots, including dots, are proportionally scaled
to 100 with largest-remainder rounding. One `Math.random()` draw selects a slot.
Consequently, a change to the raw dot weight **alone** has no effect in the
non-overflow case, while small shifts can disappear after rounding or clamping.
In overflow cases all outcomes can change when one modifier is added.

As a concrete code-level check, an isolated Jest probe used T20, ball zero,
70 batting and pace bowling ability, spin bowling ability 40, a sporting pitch,
cloudy weather, lush-green outfield, and no active tactics or skills. The
resulting 100 slots were:

| Outcome | Dot | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 | Wide | No-ball | Wicket |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Ordinary delivery | 28 | 25 | 15 | 5 | 11 | 7 | 1 | 1 | 7 |
| Armed free hit, user batting | 35 | 25 | 15 | 5 | 11 | 7 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| Armed free hit, computer batting | 28 | 25 | 15 | 5 | 11 | 7 | 1 | 1 | 7 |

These are **model probabilities for one specified situation**, not observed
cricket rates or predicted innings totals. Career control flags affect which
tactical inputs are active, so other situations will differ.

### Tactical and field behavior

- [battingTactics.js](./utils/battingTactics.js) combines approach, shot and
  target shifts, with some line, length and field interactions when opposing
  bowling tactics are provided. Certain target shifts provide unconditional
  scoring benefits before other effects; no shot-execution error is sampled.
- [bowlingTactics.js](./utils/bowlingTactics.js) combines over plan, field
  preset, line, length, variation and special-delivery shifts. A custom field
  is approximated by the preset with the most exact named-position overlap
  ([bowlingField.js](./utils/bowlingField.js)), rather than evaluated as actual
  sector coverage.
- When the user bats, [opponentField.js](./utils/opponentField.js) chooses a
  preset from format, innings phase, wickets, and chase pressure before the
  pending shot. Its position-to-sector lookup reduces singles for inner coverage,
  reduces fours and adds twos for deep coverage, and also shifts six/wicket
  weights for some attacking shots. It is an approximation, not a physical
  interception or a simulation of actual ball direction.
- When the user bowls, the preset applies generic shifts without a sampled
  computer batter's shot sector. Computer-only deliveries do not receive the
  same directional field treatment. The visible field, tactic and outcome paths
  therefore have different meanings depending on who is controlled.
- The bowling line/length matchup helpers exist, but the live delivery path only
  supplies bowling tactics for the user-controlled bowling side, and applies
  batting tactics for the user-controlled batting side. In ordinary one-sided
  control, those interactions generally do not represent an AI bowling plan
  against a user batting plan.

The twelve current presets and custom-field behavior are documented in
[field-settings.md](./field-settings.md) and
[opponent-fielding.md](./opponent-fielding.md). Preset names alone do not ensure
that a field is legal for every format or powerplay.

## Prioritized findings and improvements

### P0: Rules and state correctness

1. **Free hits depend on control, and a wide consumes one.** The resolver
   recognizes an armed free hit only when `isUserBatting`; the delivery controller
   also computes `wasFreeHit` only for user batting. An armed free hit during
   computer batting therefore does not suppress the generic wicket and is not
   cleared normally. With user batting, a wide clears it because every token
   except `nb` clears the flag. Suppressing all `W` also cannot express a
   permissible run-out. See
   [outcome calculation](./utils/controllerOutcomeUtils.js) and
   [delivery processing](./hooks/controller/controllerDeliveryEngine.js).
   **Improve:** track the free hit as an innings/ruleset state independent of
   control, retain it over wides and repeat no-balls, and allow only the
   dismissal types permitted by the chosen competition rules. Add tests for both
   sides, control changes, wide/no-ball chains, legal balls, and run-outs.
2. **A wicket on the final ball of an over can skip over completion.** For a
   user-batting wicket with a replacement available, the delivery engine waits
   for a new batter; its over-end branch is conditional on not waiting. The
   [batter-selection handler](./hooks/controller/controllerInningsCore.js) does
   not complete that deferred over transition. This code path can leave strike,
   bowler choice, and per-over counters incorrect. This is a source-level finding,
   not a separately executed reproduction in this review. **Improve:** model
   batter replacement and over completion as independent pending transitions,
   apply each exactly once, and test sixth-ball wickets, innings-ending wickets,
   and save/load while selection is pending.
3. **Dismissal commentary and statistics can contradict each other.** The engine
   samples a generic wicket, then independently chooses commentary from
   [outVoice.js](../../gameData/outVoice.js), including run-out descriptions.
   The striker is always dismissed and the bowler always receives the wicket.
   A non-striker run-out description can therefore disagree with both the
   scorecard and bowler figures. **Improve:** sample a structured dismissal first
   (`type`, dismissed batter, credited bowler if any, fielders, runs completed),
   generate commentary from it, and update stats from the same event. Cover
   caught, bowled, lbw, stumped and striker/non-striker run-out; only credit the
   bowler for eligible dismissal types.
4. **Extras and balls faced are oversimplified.** A wide or no-ball awards exactly
   one extra, with no associated bat runs, additional running, dismissal, or
   strike change. The controller increments balls faced only on legal deliveries,
   so a no-ball is not counted as faced. **Improve:** separate legality, bat
   runs, extra categories and completed runs, and apply the chosen scoring
   convention to balls faced; model byes, leg-byes, wides and no-balls with
   correct bowler attribution and possible dismissals. Test odd completed runs
   and over-end strike changes separately.
5. **Tournament ties silently favor the team batting first.**
   [controllerCompetitionFlow.js](./hooks/controller/controllerCompetitionFlow.js)
   supplies the first-innings team as the tie winner to
   [controllerTournamentUtils.js](./utils/controllerTournamentUtils.js).
   Series ties are recorded as ties, but a tied tournament match advances a team
   without an explicit competition tiebreak. **Improve:** keep the match result
   as a tie and apply an explicit configured advancement rule, such as a
   super-over where appropriate; show that rule and its result to the player.
   Test both batting orders and a tied tiebreak.

### P1: Coherence and realism of the simulation

6. **Test and first-class cricket lack their defining match structure.**
   Test is configured with two innings per team and five days, but the live
   controller has only first and second innings with an overs-based cap, then
   compares those scores like a limited-overs game. There are no four-innings
   aggregate, declarations, follow-on, time-based draws, or day progression.
   [simulatorUtils.js](../../utils/simulatorUtils.js) also applies a
   `floor(totalOvers / 5)` bowler quota to every format, including Test.
   **Improve:** use a format-specific match state machine, legal bowling
   restrictions and time/overs remaining; specify declarations, follow-on,
   innings victories, ties and draws before claiming full Test support. Until
   then, label the current mode as a simplified Test-style match.
7. **Career fixture results and synthetic scorecards are not generated from the
   live model.** [careerFixtureSimulationUtils.js](./utils/careerFixtureSimulationUtils.js)
   draws team scores from ranges, chooses the winner from those totals, and draws
   a separate random margin and runs/wickets wording. The summary can contradict
   the scores and match situation. [careerMatchStatsUtils.js](./utils/careerMatchStatsUtils.js)
   distributes batting and bowling figures independently; dismissal strings use
   names from the batting lineup rather than the opposing bowling lineup, and
   randomly perturbed bowling runs need not sum to team runs. These cards feed
   career statistics and player-of-the-match selection. **Improve:** generate
   fixtures through the same headless rules engine, or a separately calibrated
   fast model that produces a coherent event ledger and derives the result,
   scorecards and contributions from it. Test score, wickets, participants, legal
   balls, bowler attribution and result margins end to end.
8. **Outcome resolution lacks shared two-sided intent and execution.** A user
   chooses actions, but computer opponents do not consistently choose both
   batting and bowling plans in the same model. Match pressure influences AI
   field selection, not a general AI batting approach or delivery execution.
   Uniform additive target bonuses can make particular targets objectively
   preferable regardless of ball placement. **Improve:** choose AI plans from
   information available before the delivery; sample intended versus actual
   line/length, shot choice, contact quality and direction. Apply both sides'
   choices symmetrically and make risk/reward conditional on the matchup. Avoid
   letting the fielding AI see the pending shot before choosing its field.
9. **Position geometry and individual fielders do not determine outcomes.**
   Named field positions are mapped to fixed sectors and used only in the
   user-batting coverage model; custom user fields become a closest preset.
   Direction, elevation, distance, interception, catching, throwing, and fielder
   ability are not represented. **Improve:** use the actual custom/preset
   coordinates in both directions, sample a trajectory and nearest plausible
   interceptor, then resolve stopping, catch, running and boundary outcomes.
   Reflect the field shown to the user in the applied calculation, including
   handedness and legal field restrictions. Keep a lightweight approximation if
   necessary, but document its error and use one model for both sides.
10. **Player and condition differences are coarse and often discontinuous.**
    Ability differences are partitioned into bands; actual zero attributes fall
    through `||` defaults. An isolated probe confirmed that a pace batting rating
    of zero produces the same weights as 50 for the specified neutral case.
    Rain, wind, storms, pitch and home bowling affect the matchup; only
    `boundaryScoring` from the outfield profile is consumed by the resolver,
    not `fieldingSafety` or `ballPreservation`
    ([conditions](../../gameData/matchCondition.js)). There is no modeled ball
    age, fatigue, batter settling, or condition evolution. **Improve:** use
    explicit missing-value handling, continuous bounded effects and documented
    interactions; add evolving pitch/ball/dew or fatigue only where data supports
    them. Weather effects and interruptions should be format/ruleset specific.
11. **Modifiers and phase switching are hard to calibrate.** Integer slots,
    additive skill stacking, clamping, dot remainder, and abrupt format-table
    switching can hide an intended modifier or create jumps. The last 20% and
    powerplay skill timings use separate thresholds from the table switch.
    **Improve:** retain floating-point probabilities until sampling, use a
    normalized continuous parameterization with bounded interactions, and
    evaluate actual marginal effects across representative contexts. Preserve
    intended trade-offs rather than assuming every declared raw shift survives.
12. **Arcade powers and role bonuses are mixed into a realism model.** Super
    shots, special balls, captain bonuses and team-wide skills are deliberate
    gameplay features, but can stack with tactical and ability effects. Their
    balance has not been measured here. **Improve:** retain them in a versioned
    arcade ruleset; offer a separately documented realism ruleset or adjustable
    settings rather than silently removing existing player-facing mechanics.

### P2: Reproducibility, measurement and presentation

13. **Matches are difficult to replay and audit.** Randomness is drawn from
    global `Math.random()` in outcomes and other choices. The history in
    [bowlingBallEventUtils.js](./utils/bowlingBallEventUtils.js) retains only the
    most recent 60 delivery events and lacks actual trajectory, structured
    dismissal and full random/version information. **Improve:** inject a seeded
    random source through all simulation decisions, persist generator state and
    ruleset/version in saves, and retain a complete event stream or replayable
    checkpoints. Keep the compact UI history separately if desired.
14. **Player-of-the-match recommendations are heuristic, not match impact.**
    Live and synthetic scorecards use different ranking logic in
    [controllerMomUtils.js](./utils/controllerMomUtils.js); both depend on
    the accuracy of their source figures. **Improve:** first make scorecards
    consistent, then consider context-aware contribution metrics and communicate
    that recommendations are estimates, not official adjudications.

## Proposed architecture and rollout

1. **Rules kernel:** define a competition/format ruleset and a pure
   `state + event -> state` reducer. Make inning, batter-replacement,
   over-completion, and match-end transitions explicit and idempotent.
2. **Structured delivery:** represent intended and actual delivery/shot,
   direction and trajectory, runs off the bat, each extra category, legality,
   completed runs, dismissal and responsible participants. Derive commentary,
   scorecards, history and results from that record. Don't require all advanced
   categories in the first migration; do require explicit representation of
   what the engine supports.
3. **Reproducibility:** inject a seeded PRNG into outcomes, AI, commentary,
   toss and fixtures. Store the algorithm, seed/state, engine/ruleset version,
   and input choices required for deterministic replay. Handle old saves with
   explicit migration or legacy rules; never silently reroll existing results.
4. **Tactical model:** establish information boundaries, choose both AI plans,
   execute the ball and shot, then use the selected field and fielder skill to
   resolve interception and dismissal. Ensure the UI's displayed field is the
   field used in the model.
5. **Formats and unattended fixtures:** implement the limited-overs rules and
   competition tiebreaks first; implement multi-innings/time state for Test and
   first-class separately. Run unattended career fixtures headlessly through the
   same kernel, with a fast mode only if its output invariants are equivalent.
6. **Calibration and rollout:** source licensed/available ball-by-ball datasets
   and official playing conditions, record provenance and season/competition,
   split train and held-out matches by match or season, and stratify by format,
   innings phase, wickets, chase state, and available player/venue context.
   Calibrate after rule correctness, compare against a fixed baseline, and
   version and expose the selected model. Review performance and save size before
   enabling it by default. Synthetic career fixtures should be evaluated
   separately until they share the live kernel.

## Verification and measurable acceptance criteria

The following are proposed gates, not claims about the existing implementation:

### Deterministic cricket invariants

- For every generated innings, team score equals batter runs plus all recorded
  extras; team wickets equal recorded dismissals, with no dismissed batter out
  twice. Bowler wickets equal bowler-creditable dismissals. Bowler runs conceded
  equal the runs assigned to bowlers under the selected scoring rules, excluding
  byes/leg-byes and other non-bowler charges. The sum of bowler legal balls
  equals innings legal balls for ordinary completed deliveries.
- Wides and no-balls do not advance legal balls. Balls faced and strike follow
  the chosen scoring rules and completed runs, not merely legal-ball count.
  An over completes once after its sixth legal ball, including when a batter
  selection is pending; over resets, strike, bowler eligibility, and innings
  termination are each applied once.
- Test free-hit chains `no-ball -> wide -> legal ball`, `no-ball -> no-ball ->
  legal ball`, legal dismissal types on a free hit, both innings, each control
  mode and a control change. Assert dismissal attribution, legal balls, score,
  strike, and free-hit state after every event.
- Assert tournament ties remain ties until the configured tiebreak resolves
  advancement, independent of batting order. For multi-innings formats, test
  aggregate scores, declarations, follow-on, innings victories, ties and draws.
- For career fixtures, reconcile every result margin with the underlying score,
  wickets and batting order; ensure dismissal participants belong to the correct
  teams and that derived season totals equal the sum of match contributions.

### Model and replay checks

- For a fixed seed, ruleset version, initial state and user choices, two runs and
  a save/load continuation must produce identical structured events and final
  scorecards. Different seeds must be able to produce different outcomes.
- Distributions must be finite, non-negative and sum to 1 within `1e-9` before
  sampling. Test actual post-normalization effects, including overlapping
  skills, extreme abilities, zero values and all field/target combinations.
- Use paired seeds and a documented fixture matrix to compare mirrored
  user/computer roles. Given identical teams, plans, rules and conditions,
  changing only control ownership must not change rule enforcement or the
  underlying tactical calculation. AI decisions may differ only where the
  specified information or chosen plan differs.
- For field geometry, test each sector with no relevant fielder versus a
  reachable inner/deep fielder, and verify a meaningful directional shift in
  the expected scoring/catching trade-off with all else fixed. Verify actual
  positions, not just the nearest preset. Test legal field constraints by
  format and phase.

### Calibration protocol

- Publish dataset source, filters, sample sizes, rules, model version, baseline
  and random seeds. Report held-out runs per legal ball, dot/boundary/wicket and
  extra rates, over-by-over run rates, innings totals and wicket distributions,
  chase success, player figures and match results. Use the same definitions in
  data and simulation, including illegal deliveries.
- Report uncertainty: use match-level bootstrap 95% confidence intervals for
  historical and simulated estimates, and explicitly flag small strata. Set
  numerical tolerances **before** tuning from dataset quality and product goals;
  require no material held-out regression versus the documented baseline and
  investigate any stratum outside its agreed tolerance. Do not invent universal
  real-world target rates for different formats or competitions.
- Run scenario sweeps for ability differences, fatigue, conditions, field
  positions and tactics. Compare expected runs and wicket risk jointly, check
  monotonicity only where it is intended, and identify dominant strategies or
  impossible scorecards. Include performance and save/load regression tests.

## Checks performed for this analysis

- Focused existing Jest suites for outcomes, batting tactics, opponent fields,
  and over transitions: **4 suites, 35 tests passed**.
- Two temporary isolated Jest probes against the existing resolver passed:
  the free-hit/control distribution and the zero-versus-50 ability fallback.
  Their representative distributions are shown above.
- These checks establish specific current behavior, not full match realism.
  No full suite, production build, historical calibration, or sixth-ball wicket
  reproduction was run for this documentation-only analysis.
